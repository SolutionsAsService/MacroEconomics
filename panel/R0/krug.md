# Critic: Usability (Krug), round R0

**Anchor used:** Steve Krug, Don't Make Me Think: self-evident pages, scanning not reading, the trunk test (what site is this, what page, major sections, options here, where am I, how to search).

**Observed:** The inherited repo has no page and no README, so a first visitor cannot tell what it is. The planned topics (oil, inflation, ports, CFA franc, public-goods funding, crypto allocation, data notes) share no obvious subject. Crypto allocation sits beside a development-economics calculator with no stated link. Visitors will skim, not read, and will guess from labels. "Funding public goods" and "allocation strategies" are insider terms. A bold chart alone does not say what the site is for.

**Verdict:** OBJECT

**Requested change (concrete):**
1. Name the site and give a one-line tagline on every page (e.g. "Macro Explained: charts and tools on money, trade and energy"), in the header.
2. One persistent nav on all pages, in the same place, 7 items max, plain-word labels: Oil, Inflation, Ports, CFA franc, Funding public goods, Crypto strategies, About the data. Highlight the current page; logo links home.
3. Landing: tagline, one chart with a plain-English caption stating what to notice, then a card grid, one card per topic, each with a one-sentence question it answers ("Who has the oil?") and a link. No mystery meat.
4. Group the cards under two or three headings (Markets and trade; Money and policy; Tools) so the odd topics look deliberate.
5. Every page: H1 as a question or claim, a one-line takeaway before the chart, a source line and a "data notes" link. Put the calculator inputs and a worked example above the explanation.
6. Footer repeats the nav.

**Statement of meaning:** A first-time visitor should know within five seconds what the site is, which page they are on, and where each of the seven topics lives.
